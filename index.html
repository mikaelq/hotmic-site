How it works

Apple only delivers the AirPods stem-press to a process the system believes is handling a call's audio. HotMic runs exactly that kind of audio unit — but every sample it receives is rendered and immediately discarded, and it only runs while another app is already using your microphone. Outside meetings it is dormant, so it never drags your AirPods into low-quality phone-call audio.

Once HotMic has the gesture, it mutes through the best route available:

  • In-app sync — a small browser extension flips the meeting tab's own mute button, and changes made in the tab sync back. Google Meet is verified; Microsoft Teams support is in place.
  • Virtual headset — HotMic can present itself as a call-control headset (the same mechanism a Jabra or Poly headset uses), so apps that speak that protocol flip their own mute UI. This route runs on an Apple-approval-gated system capability granted to HotMic.
  • Device fallback — everywhere else, HotMic mutes the microphone itself. Works with any app; this is the honest last resort, and the menu bar tells you which route you're on.

Apps with native stem-press support, like FaceTime and recent Zoom, are left alone — HotMic stays out of the way so the built-in gesture keeps working. A global hotkey and an on-screen mute indicator round it out, and the indicator never appears in your screen shares.

Privacy

Your audio is rendered and discarded on your Mac. No audio ever leaves your device.

  • Nothing is recorded, stored, or transmitted. The audio unit exists only so macOS will deliver the mute gesture; the samples go nowhere.
  • The one network request the app makes is an update check, which sends your IP address and app version to the update server — we say this because "no network at all" would be a lie, and we don't do those.
  • No analytics, no cookies, no trackers — in the app or on this site.
  • The app is sandboxed, and the virtual-headset capability it uses is individually reviewed and granted by Apple.
