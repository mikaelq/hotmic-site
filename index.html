Many AirPods mute utilities mute at the device level: your peers stop hearing you, but the meeting app has no idea. Google Meet keeps showing you as live while you say nothing, and its own mute button now disagrees with reality: two switches, each unaware of the other. That mismatch is how hot-mic moments happen.

How it works

Apple delivers the AirPods stem press only to a process the system believes is handling a call's audio. HotMic runs that kind of audio unit. Every sample it receives is rendered and immediately discarded, and it runs only while another app is already using your microphone. Outside meetings it is dormant, so it doesn't pull your AirPods into low-quality phone-call audio. The one exception is a short practice step in setup, which you start yourself.

Once HotMic has the gesture, it mutes through the best route available:

  • In-app sync. The HotMic for Meet & Teams extension (Chrome, Edge, Brave, Arc, Chromium) flips the meeting tab's own mute button, and changes made in the tab sync back. Google Meet is verified. Microsoft Teams on the web works through the same extension. On personal Teams, a live call confirmed the mute button the extension finds and clicks; a full test with the stem press is still to come. Work and school Teams hasn't been tested in a live call yet, and some Teams calls in a language other than English may not be detected. The Teams Mac app handles the stem press itself.
  • Virtual headset. HotMic adds the HotMic Headset, which Google Meet can pair with as a call-control headset (the same mechanism a Jabra or Poly headset uses), so Meet flips its own mute button. It works with Google Meet in Chrome, not with Teams. You connect it once in Meet: More options → Settings → Audio → Call control → Connect device. macOS asks you to allow HotMic under Accessibility, which HotMic uses only to create the headset. The headset uses a system capability that Apple grants only after review, and HotMic has that grant.
  • Device fallback. Everywhere else, HotMic mutes every microphone on your Mac that can be silenced. It works with any app, though the app's own button won't change. This is the last resort, and the menu bar tells you which route you're on. You can turn it off in Settings.

HotMic leaves FaceTime, Zoom and the Microsoft Teams Mac app alone, so their own stem-press support keeps working. In Zoom, that support is in recent versions (6.4 and later) and can be switched off in Zoom's settings. If the stem press doesn't mute you in Zoom, update Zoom or use HotMic's mute shortcut, which still mutes your Mac's microphones there.

Keyboard shortcuts (mute, push-to-talk, Stealth Mute and Go to meeting, each one changeable) and an on-screen mute indicator round it out. A few more things it does:

  • Stealth Mute. Pro Off by default. While it's on, muting in a meeting (stem press, mute shortcut, menu or push-to-talk) silences every mic on your Mac that can be silenced, and the meeting keeps showing you unmuted. HotMic's menu bar icon and on-screen indicator show you as muted. ⌃⌥⇧M turns Stealth Mute on or off.
  • Push-to-talk. Pro Pick a key, hold it to talk while you're muted, and let go to mute again.
  • Pause music during meetings. Pro Off by default. HotMic pauses Music or Spotify when a call starts and, unless you changed something meanwhile, resumes what it paused when the call ends. macOS asks your permission first.
  • Go to meeting. With the HotMic for Meet & Teams extension installed, a menu item, or a shortcut you choose, brings your Meet or Teams tab and its window to the front.
  • Join Meet muted. Turn it on in the extension, and on Meet's pre-join screen it switches your mic (and, if you choose, your camera) off before you join.
  • Ask before closing a meeting tab. During a Meet or Teams call, the extension has your browser confirm before the tab closes or reloads. On by default; you can turn it off.

Privacy

Your audio is rendered and discarded on your Mac. No audio ever leaves your device.

  • Your audio is never recorded, stored, or transmitted. The audio unit exists only so macOS will deliver the mute gesture; the samples go nowhere.
  • The app's only network activity is updates: a check (automatic if you agree, or when you ask) sends your IP address and app version to the update server.
  • No analytics, cookies or trackers in the app or on this site.
  • The app is sandboxed, and the virtual-headset capability it uses is individually reviewed and granted by Apple.
